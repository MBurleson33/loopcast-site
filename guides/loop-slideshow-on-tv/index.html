How to loop a slideshow on a TV.

All you need is a laptop, an HDMI cable and a folder of pictures. Here’s how to get them repeating on the TV, full screen, for as long as you like, and how to fix the things that usually go wrong.

What you need

  • A Windows laptop or a Mac.
  • A cable from the laptop to the TV, usually HDMI. Many newer laptops only have USB-C, so you may need a USB-C to HDMI adapter.
  • Your pictures, videos or PDFs in one folder.
  • A power cable for the laptop. A slideshow that runs all day will drain a battery.

1. Connect the TV

Plug the cable into the laptop and the TV, then switch the TV to that HDMI input with its remote (often a button called Input or Source). If the TV shows “No signal”, unplug and replug the cable with the laptop awake, or try another HDMI port on the TV.

2. Set the TV as a second screen

Windows: press the Windows key and P together, then choose Extend. Mac: open System Settings › Displays and make sure the TV is set as an extended display, not a mirror.

Extending matters: the TV shows the slideshow while your laptop keeps the controls, so you can change things without the room seeing. Mirroring shows your whole desktop on the TV, including every notification.

3. Start the loop

  1. Download LoopCast (free) for Windows or Mac, or open it in your browser.
  2. Choose your folder. Each folder inside it becomes a playlist.
  3. Press Show on projector. LoopCast opens full screen on the TV.
  4. Press play. It repeats until you stop it.

4. Set the timing

Pictures stay up for the time set in LoopCast’s settings; videos play to the end and then move on. Eight to ten seconds per picture is a good start for a TV people walk past; give slides with more text longer. Want one item to play again before moving on? Press R while it’s on screen to repeat it.

Tips for a slideshow people actually read

  • Make slides 16:9 (1920×1080) so they fill the TV with no black bars.
  • Keep text big and short: a headline and one line under it. If you can’t read it from across the room on your laptop at arm’s length, it’s too small.
  • Use MP4 (H.264) for video. It plays everywhere.
  • Mix in a photo or two between text slides to catch the eye.
  • Put your logo between messages: name an image logo.png and press H to show it.

Fixing common problems

  • Black bars around the picture: the slide isn’t 16:9, or the TV is set to a different shape. Re-export at 1920×1080, and set the TV’s picture size to “Fit”, “Just scan” or “Screen fit” to stop it cropping the edges.
  • The TV goes dark after a while: the LoopCast desktop app keeps the computer awake while it plays. In a browser, set the computer’s sleep to never. Turn off the TV’s own eco or sleep timer.
  • No sound from videos: on Windows, click the speaker icon and choose the TV as the output; on a Mac, choose it in System Settings › Sound.
  • The slideshow opened on the laptop: make sure the display is extended, then in LoopCast press Find displays in Settings › Projector, or pick the TV there.

Only one screen?

If the laptop is the screen, or it’s plugged into the TV and closed, press the F key in LoopCast to fill the screen. Esc brings the controls back. For a TV that should run every day, set Settings › Projector to This screen and turn on Start the show automatically.

Questions

Can I loop a slideshow on a TV without a laptop?

Many TVs can play photos from a USB stick, but usually without PDFs, mixed video or control over timing. A laptop, mini PC or old desktop with LoopCast gives you all of that.

Why is there a black border around my slideshow?

The slides aren’t the same shape as the TV, or the TV is zooming. Export slides at 1920×1080 and set the TV’s picture size to Fit or Just scan.

Will the laptop go to sleep?

Not while the LoopCast desktop app is playing. In the browser version, set the computer’s sleep to never while the slideshow runs.

Can I change the slides without stopping the slideshow?

Yes. Add or remove files in the folder; LoopCast picks up the change the next time the loop comes around.

Does it need Wi-Fi?

No. LoopCast plays files from your computer and works offline.
