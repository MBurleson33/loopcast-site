Free digital signage for Windows and Mac.

You don’t need a subscription to put a looping sign on a TV. Here are five free options, what each one is good at, and where each one runs out.

1. Your computer’s photo slideshow

Windows Photos and macOS Photos can both play a folder of pictures as a slideshow. It’s fine for a handful of photos at home, but it isn’t built for signs: no PDFs, limited control over timing, and it doesn’t keep a second screen separate from your controls.

2. PowerPoint or Keynote on repeat

Both can loop a presentation by itself. In PowerPoint, set slide timings, then Slide Show › Set Up Slide Show › Browsed at a kiosk. In Keynote, set the presentation to Self-Playing and turn on Loop slideshow. Good when the content already lives in one deck; harder when you want to mix in videos from elsewhere, swap content during the day or switch between several loops. PowerPoint kiosk mode, step by step.

3. The TV’s USB slideshow

Many smart TVs play photos from a USB stick. It costs nothing and needs no computer, but it usually means images only, little control over timing, and walking to the TV with a stick every time something changes.

4. Free plans of cloud signage services

Hosted signage platforms manage screens from a web dashboard, usually with a small media player at each TV. Some have a free tier: Yodeck, for example, offers one screen free. They suit chains and campuses with many screens to manage from one place; most charge per screen once you grow, and need internet to manage content. How they compare with free options.

5. A dedicated looping player: LoopCast

LoopCast is free during our launch and made for one or a few screens run from a computer you already have:

  • Plays pictures, videos and PDFs from a folder, on repeat, full screen on the TV.
  • Works offline. Nothing is uploaded and there’s no account.
  • Each folder is a playlist; switch between them with a click, or on a schedule.
  • Drop in new files and they join the loop without stopping it.
  • Logo and black screens on one key, plus a clock, a message bar, a countdown and QR codes.
  • Starts by itself when the computer starts, for unattended screens.

Which should you use?

  • A few photos at home: your computer’s photo slideshow.
  • Everything is already in one deck: PowerPoint or Keynote on repeat.
  • Dozens of screens across sites, managed centrally: a cloud signage service.
  • One or a few screens, mixed media, no internet needed: LoopCast.

Getting the most from a free sign

  • Design at 1920×1080 so slides fill the TV.
  • Fewer words: a sign gets a few seconds of attention.
  • Change it often. A sign that never changes stops being read.
  • Put the folder somewhere others can reach, like a synced cloud folder, so updating isn’t one person’s job.

Questions

Is there free digital signage software for Windows?

Yes. LoopCast is free during our launch for Windows and Mac. PowerPoint’s kiosk mode and your computer’s photo slideshow are free too, with fewer features for signs.

Do I need a special player for digital signage?

No. Any Windows 10 or 11 computer or Mac with macOS 12 or later, connected to the TV, can run LoopCast.

Does free signage software need internet?

LoopCast doesn’t. It plays files from your computer. Cloud services need internet to manage content.

Can I schedule different content at different times?

Yes. LoopCast’s Settings › Schedule switches playlists at the times you set.

What file types can I use?

Images (JPG, PNG, GIF, WebP, SVG), PDFs and video (MP4, WebM, MOV). Each PDF page becomes a slide.
